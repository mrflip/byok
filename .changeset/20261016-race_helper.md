**`describeVaultDB` races its writers from two devices, under every interleaving it can make** (`safebyok/testing` grows). A new option, `makeDevices(turnstile)`, makes devices over one fresh backend -- each an adapter of its own, sharing nothing but the backend -- and passes the `turnstile` before each round trip a device makes to it (`throughTurnstile` wires one around a client's methods). The race lines (3.1 to 3.5, 3.7 and 4.5) then run under `raceEveryWay`: the moves made together, staggered a few microtasks and ticks each way round, and each move held before every one of its round trips while the other moves; four racers for the last place each held midway, then let go together. A device that cannot finish while another waits at the turnstile fails the race: the two share a lock, a queue or a connection that real devices would not, or the adapter holds a lock from one round trip to the next. `expectRoundTripsThroughTurnstile(makeDevices)` shows an adapter's factory wires it. Without `makeDevices`, the races run through one adapter as before, under the same schedules, and their titles say so.

New checks across devices: line 2.1 (a write on one device seen by the next read on another), 4.5 past the limit (a vault filled by a device allowed more), and 6.1 from another device. The push tests no longer guess that a watch is live after 50 ms: they make a write the watch hears first, and then need some call to `onChange` to read the vault as each write left it, so a push that comes too early, or not at all, fails; an unsubscribed watch is checked through several writes, not one tick. Each of these checks is also exported, runnable on its own, as `vaultDBTimingChecks`.

An adapter's own tests that pass `makeDevices` see new tests; one whose devices secretly share an adapter, a backend per device, or a lock now fails them.

**`postgresVaultDB` calls a subscriber once as its listen begins** (`safebyok-postgres`). A write that landed while the listen was starting was never heard, and a vault -- which subscribes, then reads -- could read the vault before that write and show it stale until the next one. Now it reads again once the watch is live. Through it, **`safebyok-graphql`'s `safebyokVaultChanged` sends one change as the subscription starts**. A host counting `onChange` calls sees one more at the start; the VaultDB contract's line 6.1 now says a watch that starts late calls once it is live. The schema's description of `safebyokVaultChanged` reads "whenever the vault may have changed".
