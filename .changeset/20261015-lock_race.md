A `lock()` or `destroy()` called while an `unlock()` is still under way now wins: the vault stays locked, this device remembers nothing, and the `unlock()` throws kind `vault_locked`. Before, the unlock finished afterwards and put everything back, leaving the vault unlocked and remembered though the user's last act was to lock it. A `changePassphrase()` that a lock overtakes still changes the passphrase, and leaves the vault locked. An `unlock()` whose vault is destroyed, remade or given a new passphrase on another device while the passphrase is being checked likewise takes up nothing and throws `vault_locked`, rather than holding the old vault's key against the new one. The React binding's passphrase dialog closes as if dismissed when its unlock loses this way.
