# safebyok-convex

## 0.3.0

### Minor Changes

- [#65](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/65) [`d7f2146`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/d7f21462c200c0481b92002eb01f672a3a19eacd) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **The stored format changes, with no migration** (until 1.0.0, nothing binds backward). Every stored time is now epoch milliseconds, named for its event. A lockbox keeps `registered_at` (when its boxlabel was first stored, carried forward) and `changed_at` (when its whisper last changed: storing the same whisper again writes a new revision and leaves `changed_at` standing). A vault bundle keeps `registered_at`. The ISO-string `created_at` and `updated_at` are gone from both, and the device record's `expires_at` is now `ask_passphrase_at`. The exported `Lockbox`, `LockboxSummary`, `VaultBundle` and `DeviceUnlock` schemas and types follow, and so do `safebyok/testing`'s `bundleOf` and `boxOf`, and `safebyok-convex`'s tables and validators.
  
  The host's `clock` is checked. `openVault` refuses a clock that is not `{ now() }`, or whose reading is not epoch milliseconds from 1981 to 2255 (`FarPast` to `FarFuture`), with kind `bad_option`, before it touches storage; so does a clock whose `now()` throws, its error kept as the cause. Any later call that reads it out of bounds drops the vaultKey and throws `bad_option` too, except `lockSoon()`, which cannot time its grace and so locks at once, warns through the vault's `logger` (the console by default), and returns. A reading with a fraction of a millisecond is fine, and rounded down: every stored time is whole milliseconds, and a stored record holding a fraction fails validation.
  
  `forcePassphraseReentryHours: Infinity` is still welcome, and still makes `askPassphraseAt` read `FarFuture`; it now becomes a finite window as the option is parsed, so nothing past `openVault`'s door holds `Infinity`. Likewise a storage adapter's `maxRecordBytes` or `maxVaultBytes` of `Infinity`, for no limit, is read as `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` bytes.

- [#90](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/90) [`8bdac52`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/8bdac521cc632ba18301e11e080e30e3dc808670) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **A damaged lockbox no longer locks the vault out.** A lockbox whose stored record fails the listing's check (not JSON, a malformed note or time, a record naming another boxlabel, a newer format) used to make every `refresh()`, and so `openVault`, refuse `corrupt_record`. It is now listed in the snapshot as `{ boxlabel, damaged: true }` -- the new exported `DamagedLockbox` type -- sorted among the sound lockboxes, and the vault opens and works around it. `read()` and `use()` still refuse it with `corrupt_record`; `erase()` removes it, now from any handle, and no longer reports `corrupt_record` after its own delete has landed because another lockbox was damaged.
  
  **A lockbox stored under checks a host has since narrowed is damaged, not a lockout.** One whose boxlabel or note the vault's `validators` now refuse lists as damaged too, rather than making `openVault` refuse `corrupt_record`. `erase()` removes it by the name it was stored under, whatever the boxlabel check now says; `read()` and `use()` refuse it (`bad_label` for a refused name). `store()` refuses a name the check refuses with `bad_label`, writing nothing, whether or not a lockbox stands under it; over a lockbox whose note alone is refused, it writes afresh, and the lockbox lists as sound again.
  
  **Breaking, for a host reading the snapshot's lockboxes:** `snapshot.boxes` (and a `VaultDB`'s `listBoxes`) now holds `LockboxSummary | DamagedLockbox`, each with a `damaged` field, `false` on every sound lockbox. TypeScript asks a host to check `box.damaged` before reading `note`, `changed_at` or the other summary fields. `LockboxSummary` parses with `damaged: false`.
  
  **`store()` overwrites a damaged lockbox.** Storing to a boxlabel whose record cannot be read as a lockbox (every one the snapshot lists as damaged, and one missing or mangling a ciphered field, which lists as sound) used to refuse `corrupt_record`; it now erases that record and writes a fresh lockbox, carrying neither its note nor its `registered_at`. If the write fails after the erase, the damaged record is gone and the call throws: storing again writes the lockbox afresh.
  
  **Breaking, for a storage adapter's author:** `VaultDB`'s `doListBoxes` now resolves to `StoredLockbox[]` (exported): each lockbox's record, exactly as stored, beside the boxlabel it is stored under, `{ boxlabel, lockbox }`, so a record too damaged to name itself is still listed under the boxlabel that reaches it. The in-tree adapters (`localVaultDB`, `idbVaultDB`, `convexVaultDB`) are updated; a host's own adapter maps its rows likewise. A row stored under something that is not a valid boxlabel is left out of the listing. What is stored is unchanged.

- [#79](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/79) [`648b2c4`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/648b2c4fe365bf40bb115b34c57b1d5976524431) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **`passphrase_epoch` is now `unlock_epoch`, in every stored record and every adapter, with no alias and no migration.** The counter moves whenever any way into the vault changes (a passphrase or a passkey added, removed or changed), so the old name no longer fit. It is renamed in the vault bundle (`VaultBundle`), the remembered device unlock (`DeviceUnlock`), a `VaultDB`'s `BundleExpectation`, `safebyok/testing`'s `bundleOf`, `safebyok-convex`'s tables and validators, `safebyok-indexeddb`'s checks, `safebyok-postgres`'s column (`vault-tables.sql` and the exported DDL), and `safebyok-graphql`'s schema fields. What a host with vaults already stored will see, by store:
  
  * **IndexedDB, `localVaultDB`, or a host's own `VaultDB`**: a bundle written under the old name fails validation and reads as `corrupt_record`, left as it is: a development vault is cleared from storage and made anew.
  * **Postgres**: `vault-tables.sql` creates only the tables that are missing, so running it again leaves an existing `safebyok_bundles` with its old column, and every bundle read and write fails as `storage_unavailable`, so no vault opens, until the host renames it, `ALTER TABLE safebyok_bundles RENAME COLUMN passphrase_epoch TO unlock_epoch` (which keeps every vault readable), or drops and re-creates the table.
  * **Convex**: a deployment holding bundle rows under the old name refuses the new component schema at deploy until those rows (and their vaults' lockboxes) are deleted.
  * **GraphQL**: the client and server halves must be the same version; a client of one against a server of the other fails every vault call as `storage_unavailable`.
  
  On every device, a remembered unlock written under the old name reads as absent, so that device asks for the passphrase once.

- [#79](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/79) [`d413355`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/d4133555449de0d0b883a35eb020cbcea2ae04d1) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **Fingerprint unlock: a vault's ways in may include passkeys** (a fingerprint, a face, a phone, a security key), through the WebAuthn PRF extension. A new unlock envelope, `{ method: 'passkey', unlock_id, unlocklabel, credential_id, prf_salt_b64, vault_key_wrapped }`, wraps the vaultKey under HKDF-SHA256 of the passkey's 32-byte PRF answer. Nothing already stored changes shape: a bundle written before reads as it did.
  
  The core takes the answer as a `PasskeyProof` (`{ credentialId, prfSaltB64, prfBytes }`) and never calls WebAuthn. `vault.addPasskey(proof, passkey, { unlocklabel })` adds one and returns its `unlock_id`; `vault.unlock(passkey)` opens with one, with no key derivation, remembered on the device as a passphrase unlock is. `addPassphrase` and `removeUnlock` now take a passphrase or a passkey's answer as proof, so a passkey can set a new passphrase for a user who forgot theirs; `changePassphrase` still takes the passphrase it changes. A vault always keeps one passphrase: `removeUnlock` refuses removing the last with `last_unlock`, though passkeys remain. The snapshot's `unlocks` lists a passkey's `credential_id` and `prf_salt_b64` too (`UnlockSummary` is now a union by `method`). New exports: `PasskeyEnvelope`, `PasskeySaltBytes` and the type `PasskeyProof`; `UnlockMethodVals` gains `passkey`. New error kinds, both conditions: `wrong_passkey` and `passkey_unavailable`. `safebyok/utils` exports the base64url codec, `toB64` and `fromB64`. `safebyok/testing` gains `passkeyOf` (a fake passkey's answer) and `passkeyEnvelopeOf`.
  
  `safebyok-react` adds `createPasskey()` and `askPasskey()`, which make the WebAuthn calls (and import no React), `passkeysAvailable()`, and `PasskeyUnlockButton`; `PassphraseModal` offers "Use a passkey" for a vault that has one. `safebyok-convex`'s `UnlockEnvelope` validator gains a `PasskeyEnvelope` member, an additive schema change.

- [#79](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/79) [`53b1f1a`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/53b1f1aabea16102dc7bfb6187410369e05b2e53) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **Every storage adapter declares how many lockboxes it holds in one vault, and the Postgres server enforces its own.**
  
  * **Breaking, for adapter authors:** `VaultDB` gains an abstract `maxLockboxes`, the most lockboxes the backend holds in one vault, or `Infinity` for no limit. A custom adapter adds `readonly maxLockboxes = Infinity`, or its backend's real limit (a large whole number where the limit must cross a wire or be stored). `openVault` refuses, with kind `bad_option`, an adapter that declares none.
  * `openVault`'s `maxLockboxes` defaults to the smaller of `MaxLockboxes` (100) and the adapter's own, and is refused with kind `bad_option` when it is larger than the adapter's.
  * `localVaultDB` and `idbVaultDB` declare `Infinity`; `convexVaultDB` declares the component's own limit, 8,000.
  * `postgresVaultDB` takes a `maxLockboxes` option (default 1000; for no practical limit pass a large number, such as `1_200_300_400_500_600`, since `Infinity` cannot reach Postgres) and enforces it, as a best effort, inside the statement that inserts a new lockbox, refusing one past it with kind `vault_full`; a replacement never is. Writers racing for the last places may each find room and all land, a few past the limit, which the VaultDB contract allows. It keeps no count in the tables: `safebyok_bundles` has no `lockbox_count` column and nothing counts lockboxes by trigger.
  * `graphqlVaultDB(client, { maxLockboxes })` declares what the server's VaultDB enforces (default 1000, the Postgres VaultDB's default); the server's `vault_full` crosses as itself.

- [#79](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/79) [`b49c1f4`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/b49c1f406d0c40521f0e1740e6221f2309c9e882) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **The stored vault bundle changes shape, with no migration** (until 1.0.0, nothing binds backward). A bundle's `key_deriver_label`, `passphrase_salt_b64` and `vault_key_passphrase_wrapped` are replaced by `unlocks`, a list of one to `MaxUnlocks` (8) unlock envelopes, each `{ method: 'passphrase', unlock_id, unlocklabel, key_deriver_label, salt_b64, vault_key_wrapped }`, each wrapping the one vaultKey under its own passphrase and salt. A bundle written before this fails validation and reads as `corrupt_record`, left as it is: a development vault is destroyed and made anew. A Convex deployment holding such bundle rows refuses the new component schema at deploy until those rows (and their vaults' lockboxes) are deleted. Lockboxes and the device record are unchanged.
  
  A vault may now have several passphrases. `vault.addPassphrase(passphrase, newPassphrase, { unlocklabel })` adds one and returns its `unlock_id`; `vault.removeUnlock(passphrase, unlockId)` removes one, never the last (kind `last_unlock`); `changePassphrase(old, new, { unlockId? })` changes only the passphrase `old` opens, keeping its id and label. Each asks for a passphrase the vault opens with, even while unlocked, and moves `unlock_epoch`, so every other device locks. `unlock(passphrase, { unlockId? })` tries each passphrase in turn, one key derivation apiece, or only the one named. The snapshot lists the ways in, locked or not, as `unlocks` (`UnlockSummary`: `method`, `unlock_id`, `unlocklabel`). New exports: `MaxUnlocks`, `PassphraseEnvelope`, `UnlockEnvelope`, `UnlockMethodVals`, and the types `UnlockMethod` and `UnlockSummary`. New error kinds, all conditions: `absent_unlock`, `too_many_unlocks`, `last_unlock`; a taken `unlocklabel` is `bad_label`.
  
  `safebyok/testing`'s `bundleOf` builds the new shape, and a new `envelopeOf` builds one fake passphrase envelope. `safebyok-convex`'s `VaultBundle` validator and table follow, with new `PassphraseEnvelope` and `UnlockEnvelope` validators. `safebyok-react`'s empty snapshot carries `unlocks: []`.

- [#115](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/115) [`3304864`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/3304864f594995dad682d1048059e3b22f1c8dc6) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - A vault now locks itself when its user signs out. A new error kind, `identity_revoked`, is storage saying that the user a vault was opened for is no longer the one signed in -- signed out, or another user signed in -- and the vault meets it by locking at once, as `lock()` does: its vaultKey dropped, this device's remembered unlock forgotten, the snapshot `locked`. It erases nothing stored, and the call that met the refusal throws it. A background refresh that meets it locks all the same, and tells the logger. `openVault`'s new `whenIdentityRevoked` option chooses `'lock'` (the default) or `'stay'`, which only throws, leaving the vault as before; `IdentityRevokedResponseVals` lists the two. `VaultProvider` passes the option through.
  
  **Breaking, for code that branches on `not_permitted`**: `safebyok-convex`'s functions and `safebyok-postgres`'s `postgresVaultDB` (and so `safebyok-graphql`'s resolvers over it) now refuse a signed-out caller, and a caller who is not the vault's user, with `identity_revoked` where they said `not_permitted`. Branch on `identity_revoked` to send the user to sign in. `not_permitted` stays, for a signed-in user refused one call by a backend of your own, and locks nothing. Convex's `SafebyokErrorKindVals` is `['identity_revoked', 'vault_full']`. Over a `ConvexClient`, a failed `watchVault` now makes the vault look again (as over a `ConvexReactClient` it already did), so a sign-out locks even an idle vault.
  
  A vault opened before its backend knows who is signed in meets `identity_revoked` at once, and forgets its remembered unlock: open it only once your auth is ready (with Convex in React, once `useConvexAuth()` says so).

The refusal's message points at the deployment's logs, which have the full error, or, for trouble before the call left, the browser console. A host that read `cause` for debugging reads those instead.

A storage adapter of your own may throw through it; safebyok's own refusal for a host schema that throws already does.

- [#120](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/120) [`6acb67d`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/6acb67d28e995f0b47a1c0950b241d0c81693571) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **Stored shape: every vault now records which vaultKey it stands under, and each device refuses an older copy of a vault it has already seen newer.** No vault written before this reads: no compatibility before 1.0. `openVault` refuses such a vault as `damaged_bundle`; `eraseDamagedVault(opts)` clears it (it knows `safebyok-vault-v1` as an older format, not a newer one), and the vault can then be made anew. Its lockboxes list as damaged until then.
  
  - **`safebyok`**:
    - The bundle's format is `safebyok-vault-v2`. It gains `vault_key_epoch` (1 for a vault's first vaultKey) and `prior_vault_key` (absent until a vault's vaultKey is replaced). Each unlock envelope gains `vault_key_epoch`, `device_id` (a random id the device that added it keeps for the vault) and `registered_at`, all bound into its wrap. An envelope at another `vault_key_epoch` than its bundle's is passed over as damaged (`damaged_unlock`).
    - The lockbox's format is `safebyok-lockbox-v2`. It gains `vault_key_epoch`, bound into both its encryptions, so `LockboxSummary` and the snapshot's `boxes` carry it too. A lockbox at another epoch than its bundle's reads as `damaged_lockbox`.
    - `LockboxExpectation` gains `vault_key_epoch`. `VaultDB.updateBox` lands a lockbox only while the bundle stands at that epoch (`VAULTDB.md` line 3.8), and refuses as `bad_option` a lockbox whose own epoch is not the expected one. An adapter of your own compares it in its `doUpdateBox`, beside `vault_id` and `revision_id`; `describeVaultDB` tests it, and its new option `comparesVaultKeyEpoch: false` holds that test pending for an adapter that does not yet. `localVaultDB` compares it; the IndexedDB, Convex and Postgres adapters (and GraphQL's recipe, over Postgres) do not yet. Nothing in this release replaces a vaultKey either, so every vault stands at epoch 1 and the comparison has nothing yet to refuse.
    - `DeviceDB` gains `readMark`, `writeMark` and `eraseMark`, which a host's own `DeviceDB` must implement (`openVault` refuses one without them as `bad_option`), and `eraseAll` erases marks too. A device mark (`DeviceMark`, format `safebyok-mark-v1`) holds the device's id for the vault and the highest `unlock_epoch` and `vault_key_epoch` it has seen. `memoryDeviceDB` and `idbDeviceDB` keep them; `Ckeys.mark` and `Ckeys.isMark` name their rows.
    - New error kind `rolled_back`, a condition: this device has already seen a newer copy of the vault than storage offers now, at a higher `unlock_epoch` or `vault_key_epoch`. It is refused at open, unlock, every change to the ways in and every refresh, and nothing is taken from it. A host whose storage was restored from an older copy on purpose accepts it on a device by `deviceDB.eraseMark(scope)` and opening again (the README's *A vault rolled back*). `destroy()` and `eraseDamagedVault` erase the mark.
  - **`safebyok-convex`**: the component's validators carry the new fields and formats, so a deployment holding records written before this no longer matches its schema: clear the component's tables before deploying it.
  - **`safebyok-postgres`**: `safebyok_bundles` gains `vault_key_epoch` and `prior_vault_key`, and `safebyok_lockboxes` gains `vault_key_epoch`. `VaultTablesSql` creates only missing tables, so drop and recreate tables made before this.
  - **`safebyok-graphql`**: the schema carries the new fields (`prior_vault_key` as the scalar `SafebyokPriorVaultKey`), and a lockbox expectation's `vault_key_epoch`.
