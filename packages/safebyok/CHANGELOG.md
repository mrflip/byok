- [#65](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/65) [`d7f2146`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/d7f21462c200c0481b92002eb01f672a3a19eacd) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **The stored format changes, with no migration** (until 1.0.0, nothing binds backward). Every stored time is now epoch milliseconds, named for its event. A lockbox keeps `registered_at` (when its boxlabel was first stored, carried forward) and `changed_at` (when its whisper last changed: storing the same whisper again writes a new revision and leaves `changed_at` standing). A vault bundle keeps `registered_at`. The ISO-string `created_at` and `updated_at` are gone from both, and the device record's `expires_at` is now `ask_passphrase_at`. The exported `Lockbox`, `LockboxSummary`, `VaultBundle` and `DeviceUnlock` schemas and types follow, and so do `safebyok/testing`'s `bundleOf` and `boxOf`, and `safebyok-convex`'s tables and validators.
  
  The host's `clock` is checked. `openVault` refuses a clock that is not `{ now() }`, or whose reading is not epoch milliseconds from 1981 to 2255 (`FarPast` to `FarFuture`), with kind `bad_option`, before it touches storage; so does a clock whose `now()` throws, its error kept as the cause. Any later call that reads it out of bounds drops the vaultKey and throws `bad_option` too, except `lockSoon()`, which cannot time its grace and so locks at once, warns through the vault's `logger` (the console by default), and returns. A reading with a fraction of a millisecond is fine, and rounded down: every stored time is whole milliseconds, and a stored record holding a fraction fails validation.
  
  `forcePassphraseReentryHours: Infinity` is still welcome, and still makes `askPassphraseAt` read `FarFuture`; it now becomes a finite window as the option is parsed, so nothing past `openVault`'s door holds `Infinity`. Likewise a storage adapter's `maxRecordBytes` or `maxVaultBytes` of `Infinity`, for no limit, is read as `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` bytes.

- [#90](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/90) [`8bdac52`](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/commit/8bdac521cc632ba18301e11e080e30e3dc808670) Thanks [@mrflip](https://github.com/mrflip)! - **A damaged lockbox no longer locks the vault out.** A lockbox whose stored record fails the listing's check (not JSON, a malformed note or time, a record naming another boxlabel, a newer format) used to make every `refresh()`, and so `openVault`, refuse `corrupt_record`. It is now listed in the snapshot as `{ boxlabel, damaged: true }` -- the new exported `DamagedLockbox` type -- sorted among the sound lockboxes, and the vault opens and works around it. `read()` and `use()` still refuse it with `corrupt_record`; `erase()` removes it, now from any handle, and no longer reports `corrupt_record` after its own delete has landed because another lockbox was damaged.
  
  **A lockbox stored under checks a host has since narrowed is damaged, not a lockout.** One whose boxlabel or note the vault's `validators` now refuse lists as damaged too, rather than making `openVault` refuse `corrupt_record`. `erase()` removes it by the name it was stored under, whatever the boxlabel check now says; `read()` and `use()` refuse it (`bad_label` for a refused name). `store()` refuses a name the check refuses with `bad_label`, writing nothing, whether or not a lockbox stands under it; over a lockbox whose note alone is refused, it writes afresh, and the lockbox lists as sound again.
  
  **Breaking, for a host reading the snapshot's lockboxes:** `snapshot.boxes` (and a `VaultDB`'s `listBoxes`) now holds `LockboxSummary | DamagedLockbox`, each with a `damaged` field, `false` on every sound lockbox. TypeScript asks a host to check `box.damaged` before reading `note`, `changed_at` or the other summary fields. `LockboxSummary` parses with `damaged: false`.
  
  **`store()` overwrites a damaged lockbox.** Storing to a boxlabel whose record cannot be read as a lockbox (every one the snapshot lists as damaged, and one missing or mangling a ciphered field, which lists as sound) used to refuse `corrupt_record`; it now erases that record and writes a fresh lockbox, carrying neither its note nor its `registered_at`. If the write fails after the erase, the damaged record is gone and the call throws: storing again writes the lockbox afresh.
  
  **Breaking, for a storage adapter's author:** `VaultDB`'s `doListBoxes` now resolves to `StoredLockbox[]` (exported): each lockbox's record, exactly as stored, beside the boxlabel it is stored under, `{ boxlabel, lockbox }`, so a record too damaged to name itself is still listed under the boxlabel that reaches it. The in-tree adapters (`localVaultDB`, `idbVaultDB`, `convexVaultDB`) are updated; a host's own adapter maps its rows likewise. A row stored under something that is not a valid boxlabel is left out of the listing. What is stored is unchanged.
